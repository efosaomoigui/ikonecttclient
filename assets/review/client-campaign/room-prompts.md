# Room plate prompts

Built-in imagegen, 26 October 2026. The original source flyers were inspected first. Both outputs are photographic **background plates only**, with no people. Original subject pixels are composited over them by `scripts/prepare-client-campaign-scenes.cjs`. Installed native PNG masters are `client-family-scene-v2.png` and `client-individual-scene-v2.png` in this folder.

## Family room

Input/edit reference: `family-flyer.png`. Output copied into this project as `family-room-plate.png`. Transparency: false.

Use case: precise-object-edit / background plate. The input is an official campaign FLYER. Create ONLY the clean photographic living-room ENVIRONMENT that will sit behind the original family, who will be composited back separately without altering any of their pixels. Therefore there must be NO PEOPLE in this background plate. Remove all four people, all logos, all text, all headlines, all pricing, all cards, all icons, all illustrations and advertising graphics from this image. Reconstruct the plausible warm contemporary living room from the photographed background: a real beige/light grey woven fabric sofa spanning the lower third, substantial comfortable back cushions, a softly lit cream wall, a softly defocused window with curtains at far left and one elegant warm floor lamp at far right. Photography, NOT flat graphic, NOT isolated furniture on a blank seamless studio background. Camera at seated eye level, front view to the sofa. Composition: LANDSCAPE 2:1, a tight upper sofa and room portrait, sofa backrest top at about 55% of canvas height and seat mostly cropped beneath the lower edge; plenty of actual room context in upper half and side edges. Gentle daylight from upper left, matching the family flyer's frontal soft lighting. Warm natural off-white tones, neutral ivory upholstery; realistic texture, clean uncluttered interior. The original family will occupy x=5%-88%, y=25%-100%; keep sofa arms visible at both outer edges. Do NOT generate any human, body part, portrait, face or silhouette. No brand/logo, text, pricing, watermark, wifi icon, network lines, red graphic shapes. Finished premium editorial photographic clean plate, no transparency.

## Individual room

Input/edit reference: `individual-flyer.png`. Output copied into this project as `individual-room-plate.png`. Transparency: false.

Use case: precise-object-edit / background plate. Input is an official campaign FLYER, environment reference only. Create a clean PHOTOGRAPHIC LIVING ROOM BACKGROUND PLATE to composite the exact original man and distinctive red chair over later, so DO NOT include any people OR any red chair in your output. Remove all text, logos, plan-price cards, headlines, icons, graphics, footer and all people from the flyer. No replacement subjects. Rebuild the softly lit elegant living room suggested by its blurred cream interior: a warm off-white plaster wall, softly diffused window daylight from left, soft neutral rug and a subtle dark timber floor at the very bottom, and a gently out-of-focus warm lamp and low console at far left. The original man in his red chair will occupy the RIGHT three quarters of the frame, almost to bottom edge, with his head near x65%, y17%, so leave that right area clear and the room details to the LEFT edge and upper margins. Seat-level camera angle, portrait-photo crop through the lower seating, natural focal perspective and soft depth of field. LANDSCAPE 3:2. Real interior photography with warm neutral shades that suit a bold red chair and dark clothing. NO blank studio backdrop, NO isolated cutouts, NO new furniture where the original red chair will be, NO people, body parts, faces, silhouettes, writing, branding, wifi symbols, network cables, graphics, watermark or advertising. Natural, premium room context with actual environmental texture, uncluttered.

## Limitations

The rooms are rebuilt surroundings, not recovered untouched original locations. The original small flyers still limit subject resolution. No obscured human anatomy is reconstructed. The composited, opaque master/export records specify subject offsets and verify that all fully opaque native subject RGB pixels remain unchanged.
